The operation behind the dots

Every dot on this map is a landing — and a landing is never just a landing. Here’s what has to happen at the other end:

  • Cars waiting on the ramp. When the jet touches down, there’s ground transport already there — rental cars, drivers, a way off the airport and to the next meeting. Nobody’s standing around calling an Uber.
  • Fuel, crews, and a place to sleep. Each stop means fueling, a crew, hotels, catering — the whole machine moves with the plane.
  • The jets go where the business is. Edmonton, nine days before a new store opens. Ottawa, on the way. Napa and Santa Rosa. Then straight across the Pacific to Seoul. This isn’t wandering — it’s the company’s calendar, at 41,000 feet.
  • And sometimes they just go dark. The jets’ transponders go silent for hours or days — hangared, or out of coverage — and then they pop up on the other side of the world. That’s how Napa turns into Seoul overnight.

Four jets. Nine stops. About 12,800 miles. One week. That’s the scale of it.
